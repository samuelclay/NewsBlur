Since 2009, NewsBlur has only spoken English. Plenty of you read feeds in other languages every day, and you've still had to find your way around the reader in English. Starting today, NewsBlur's interface is available in 21 languages on the web, iOS, and Android. Menus, buttons, settings, dialogs, and the first-time setup all appear in your language, picked up automatically from your browser or phone.

<img src="/assets/native-language-reader-ja.jpg" alt="The NewsBlur web reader in Japanese, with an English article from Ars Technica open" style="width: 100%;border: 1px solid rgba(0,0,0,0.1);margin: 24px auto;display: block;">

Your stories stay exactly as they were published. NewsBlur translates its own interface, not the feeds you read. Feed titles, story text, comments, and usernames all stay in their original language. The screenshot above is NewsBlur in Japanese, reading an English article from Ars Technica.

### Picked up automatically

You don't need to do anything to switch. On the web, NewsBlur uses your browser's language. On iOS and Android, it uses your phone's language list. If your language isn't one of the 21, everything stays in English.

Because a new language can be a surprise, NewsBlur tells you which one it picked the first time you open it. The dialog shows the same message side by side, in the language it detected and in English. One button keeps it, the other switches back to English. That way you can always get back, even if NewsBlur guessed wrong because you're traveling, living abroad, or using a VPN.

<img src="/assets/native-language-dialog.png" alt="NewsBlur's language dialog with the same message in German and English side by side, and buttons to use German or English" style="width: 80%;border: 1px solid rgba(0,0,0,0.1);margin: 24px auto;display: block;">

If NewsBlur picked English, you'll see a shorter version with every language listed in its own script, in case you'd rather read in another one. New accounts get the same choice as the very first step of setup.

### Choose your language on the web

You can change your language anytime in **Preferences**, right at the top under **Language**. **Automatic** follows your browser, or you can pick any language. If you're signed out, there's a language menu at the top of the home page, so the sign in and sign up forms are in your language before you even have an account.

<img src="/assets/native-language-welcome-fr.png" alt="The NewsBlur home page in French, with the language menu at the top left set to Auto, Français" style="width: 100%;border: 1px solid rgba(0,0,0,0.1);margin: 24px auto;display: block;">

### Choose your language on iOS

On iPhone and iPad, open **Preferences** and choose **Language** at the top. Pick a language and the app switches right away, no restart needed.

{% include mobile-release-phone.html image="/assets/ios-native-language-preferences.png" alt="NewsBlur Preferences on iPhone in Spanish, with Idioma set to Español" %}

Everything follows along, from your feed list to the story controls.

{% include mobile-release-phone.html image="/assets/ios-native-language-feeds.png" alt="The NewsBlur feed list on iPhone in Spanish" %}

### Choose your language on Android

On Android, it's the first row in **Settings**. The app switches as soon as you choose, and Android also lets you set NewsBlur's language from the system's per-app language settings.

{% include mobile-release-phone.html image="/assets/android-native-language-settings.png" alt="NewsBlur Settings on a Galaxy phone in German, with Sprache set to Deutsch" %}

Menus and numbers follow your language too.

{% include mobile-release-phone.html image="/assets/android-native-language-menu.png" alt="The feed options menu on Android in German" %}

### One language, everywhere you read

When you pick a language, it's saved to your account, so your phone and the web stay in sync. If you leave it on Automatic, each device follows its own language settings. That's handy if your laptop is in English and your phone is in Spanish.

### Help me get the words right

Mention the language, where you saw it, and what it should say, and I'll get it fixed.
