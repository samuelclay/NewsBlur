{% trans "Two indie readers, different philosophies" %}

{% trans "NewsBlur and Feedbin are both independent, privacy-respecting RSS readers built by small teams. They share a commitment to open source and a belief that RSS should be simple and user-controlled. The key difference is depth: Feedbin focuses on a clean, minimal reading experience. NewsBlur adds intelligence training, AI features, social sharing, and native mobile apps on top of a similarly clean interface. If you want the simplest possible reader, Feedbin is excellent. If you want more power without sacrificing usability, NewsBlur delivers." %}

{% trans "Intelligence training: the biggest differentiator" %}

{% trans "NewsBlur's intelligence training lets you highlight or hide stories based on author, tag, title keywords, full text content, URLs, and regex patterns. Over time, your feeds become personalized. Stories are color-coded green (focus), yellow (neutral), and red (hidden). Feedbin has no equivalent filtering system. You manage reading through folders, tags, and manual triage. For high-volume reading across hundreds of feeds, NewsBlur's training is a major advantage." %}

{% trans "Native apps vs. third-party ecosystem" %}

{% trans "NewsBlur builds and maintains its own native iOS and Android apps with full feature parity, including intelligence training, blurblog sharing, and text view. Feedbin relies entirely on third-party apps like Reeder and NetNewsWire. Both approaches work, but NewsBlur's native apps mean every feature is available on mobile from day one, without waiting for a third-party developer to add support." %}

{% trans "Free tier and pricing" %}

{% trans "NewsBlur offers a free plan with up to 64 sites and a 30-day premium trial for every new account. Feedbin has no free tier and charges $50/year for a single plan. NewsBlur Premium at $36/year is more affordable and includes up to 1,024 sites. The Archive plan at $99/year adds permanent story storage, Ask AI, daily briefings, web feeds, and regex classifiers. Both readers let you export via OPML at any time." %}
