# Premium Team

Start with the editor who owns a beat and three colleagues who take turns covering it. Give them one source list, a choice of individual or shared unread counts, and an answer to a question their reader ought to settle in seconds. **“Who’s covering this, and does their alert work?”** Include company SSO on the four-person offer at the first public paid launch. Test **$79 per workspace per month** before publishing a price. Feeder already sells shared unread state, and Inoreader already sells SSO and managed folders to small teams. The reason to choose NewsBlur should be how well a desk handles a real story from discovery through handoff. ## How four people would cover a beat

Imagine a policy desk following regulator notices. The owner creates **Policy Desk**, invites three named colleagues and adds a regulator’s RSS feed to **Agency releases**. A new story appears for all four. On the afternoon shift, Jo claims it and passes it to Lee for follow-up. The editor can see the owner of that story and test the next shift’s alerts. Meanwhile Jo’s own subscriptions, saved stories, personal reading history and personal notification preferences belong to Jo.

| Where the user acts | Proposed behavior |
| --- | --- |
| **Manage team folder** | Add a public RSS/Atom feed or preview an OPML import. Assign the folder to everyone, including future members, or to named members. Show “Visible to 4 people” before saving. Add, rename, move or remove a source once and every authorized reader gets the new revision. Removing the team copy preserves a personal follow. Show team-owned folders under a named team root beside Personal. |
| **Folder reading mode** | Default to **Individual reads** for research folders. **Shared reads** suits a wire desk. Opening or marking a story read *in that shared folder* clears the unread dot there for everyone. Marking it unread restores it there for everyone. The same URL can sit in a personal feed or another team folder with its own state. A mode-change preview gives the affected count and a starting point for the new mode. Apply the switch to new arrivals and retain the earlier items’ original behavior, including their mode label. Confirm “Mark read for all 4” for bulk actions. |
| **Story and shift coverage** | Put a people icon and **Shared reads · 4 assigned** beside the folder title. Give each story a separate action to **Claim**, **Hand off** or **Mark handled**. Display “Claimed by Jo” with time, and preserve that claim when someone opens the story or marks it unread. A simultaneous claim shows the colleague who won it. Start with a primary and backup per folder and a manual shift handoff. Add recurring shifts, time zones and holiday exceptions after desks use that handoff regularly. |
| **Alert rules and coverage** | A manager selects a folder, optional source or keyword, recipients, channel, urgency and fallback. Preview a matching story and its intended recipients before enabling. Display a coverage table with one row per person and rule. Each row gives the assigned route, last setup signal, last test outcome and time, plus an action such as **Send test** or **Request setup**. Member instructions open on that member’s device. A recipient can explicitly acknowledge a test or live story. |
| **Personal settings** | Keep private folders and alert settings in the personal dialog. Show the source of a team policy and who can edit it. Managers see team activity and the minimum route status needed to fix a gap. Only the member sees their personal subscriptions, private address or device details beyond what they explicitly share for work. |

Give each folder banner and story badge a text label, keyboard action and accessible name. On a small screen show the team name, read mode and current claimant first, with membership and routing details one tap away. Use the same labels in search results, deep links and bulk confirmation. An unlabeled “read” action that updates four screens would undermine trust in both views.

**What a verified alert means.** The coverage table can show policy assigned, setup missing, test queued, provider accepted, failed, last device report stale, or acknowledged by the person. Show the evidence time for each. Provider acceptance is enough to say “the email service accepted this at 14:03.” A person’s explicit response supports “Lee acknowledged at 14:04.” Device permission and delivery receipts vary by channel. Let the manager retest or use the fallback when evidence is stale. Show an uncovered-folder banner when no assigned responder has a usable route.

## What the workspace owns and pays for

**Identity.** The owner creates the workspace and adds managers or readers. Owners control billing, domain configuration and transfer of ownership. Managers edit team sources, assignments and routing. Readers consume and claim work. Start with expiring invitations for the private pilot. For the public four-person offer, support a DNS-verified domain, one SAML identity provider, a test sign-in, enforced workspace SSO, and just-in-time provisioning for people explicitly assigned at the IdP. Bind identities to the issuer and stable subject. Linking an existing personal account requires proof of control. A domain match alone grants no access. A contractor can use an explicitly assigned IdP guest identity. Keep an audited recovery path and require a successor before the last owner leaves.

At offboarding, revoke team sessions and pending invites, reassign open work, and recheck access on queued deliveries and deep links. Personal memberships, subscriptions and individually purchased plans survive. Without SCIM, document how to disable future IdP sign-in and immediately remove the active workspace membership in NewsBlur. Test bounded SSO session lifetime and reauthentication on the web and on mobile. Later contract options can cover SCIM deactivation, group mapping, multiple domains, procurement, retention agreements and audit export.

**Price experiment.** Quote real teams the two alternatives below at the same four-person starting price. Give both entry offers the same monitoring allowance so the comparison tests what happens when a fifth colleague joins. All amounts and capacities are **test offers**. Choose one public meter after the pilot. Count distinct team sources once per workspace across its folders. Ordinary public feeds can use the normal fetch schedule. Reserve the tighter limits for priority polling and alert fan-out.

| Proposed monthly offer | Four total people | Owner plus four teammates | Twelve people, same sources |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Seat bundle with four named people included, $12 for each additional person | $79 | $91 | $175 |
| Unlimited named people, priced on capacity. Test $79 for 25 priority feeds and 5,000 recipient deliveries/month, or $149 for 100 priority feeds and 20,000 deliveries/month. | $79 at entry capacity | $79 at entry capacity | $79 at entry capacity |

The initial recommendation is **the capacity offer** if costs and buyer behavior support it. For example, a four-person desk tracking 300 ordinary feeds and prioritizing 12 fits the $79 proposal. A desk prioritizing 60 would fit the $149 proposal, even if both desks have four readers. A manager can invite a backup or another department without requesting a seat purchase. Make clear that four is the example team size under that offer. A customer exceeding 25 priority feeds can still use ordinary feeds at standard cadence or select the higher allowance. Define a delivery as one matched story to one eligible person or shared destination on one channel. Four people on two channels can use eight deliveries. Deduplicate overlapping rules and exclude retries and setup tests from the buyer’s meter. Show usage, warnings and the forecast before purchase. At 80% of the delivery allowance, notify the billing contact and show which rules consume it. At 100%, keep incoming stories in the team inbox, pause metered outbound alerts, and show a persistent warning to the desk. Reserve a separate operational notice for the manager. Resume new outbound alerts on upgrade or at the next billing period and offer a digest of the gap. Require opt-in before an overage charge.

Measure incremental polls and proxy charges for priority sources, rule evaluations, channel attempts, storage, SSO provider fees and onboarding/support time. For example, at a proposed 70% contribution margin the $79 plan leaves **$23.70 per workspace per month** for attributable costs. Ten desks following one public feed can share its fetch cost, while a burst of stories and hundreds of recipients can exhaust delivery capacity quickly. Model both an ordinary month and a burst before accepting the quote. Compare the four-person result with Feeder and Inoreader, and with NewsBlur’s own $29/month personal Pro plan, whose feature bundle serves a different job. [Feeder pricing](https://feeder.co/pricing) · [Inoreader pricing](https://www.inoreader.com/pricing/enterprise) · [NewsBlur Pro](https://www.newsblur.com/pricing/pro)

**Billing.** Give the team its own web checkout, Stripe customer/subscription, billing contact and invoice history. Membership grants features in team context. A reader can retain a separately paid personal plan, and a free reader can join without an upgrade. An owner transfer preserves the workspace subscription. A past-due payment triggers notice and a time-limited grace period. Preview a downgrade, retain existing folders, and ask the owner to choose which priority sources stay accelerated. Cancellation at term ends monitoring, allows a disclosed export window and preserves personal billing. Route and deduplicate provider webhooks by workspace subscription, including late or reordered events. Offer an invoice/procurement inquiry for buyers who need a contract.

## What NewsBlur can build on

The current reader already fetches public feeds into a common story corpus. Its subscription rows, JSON folder trees and Redis read markers attach to individuals. The notification worker takes a feed ID and new-story count, selects the latest hashes, skips readers inactive for 90 days, and caps a user’s batch at three. The web channel publishes to Redis, while Android creates local alerts during sync. Those details matter to a desk expecting every matching notice, even when the app is closed. ## How to earn the first paying desks

Recruit **six four-person desks** for a proposed four-week private pilot, split between newsrooms and regulatory or industry-watch teams. Start with each desk’s own sources and the last story it missed or covered twice. Watch its manager import a folder, add a source visible to three colleagues, test a route, correct a missing recipient and hand off one live story. Measure time to first verified route and ask the manager to compare the result with their current tool. Offer both pricing models with an actual paid continuation choice. Record the reason for each refusal.

As a proposed decision gate, look for four of six desks to activate within a week (two named colleagues joined, one live folder and a successful recipient test), three to use claims or handoffs in at least three pilot weeks, and three to elect a paid continuation at a quoted price. Verify margin and the alert target independently. If teams pay for managed folders but seldom use claims, lead the next cohort with shared subscriptions and alert setup. If more colleagues join under the capacity offer and margins hold under burst traffic, publish it. Use those pilot results to decide whether to recruit a second cohort.

Place **“Need a team plan?”** on the public Premium comparison and in the existing web upgrade dialog, leading to a dedicated Premium Team page with price, included SSO and a short demo. Test two search-intent pages, **“Shared RSS reader for newsrooms”** and **“Regulatory news alerts for teams.”** Show a real source, two teammates handing off one story and an example of a failed route repaired. Use **“Try Premium Team”** for the self-serve path and **“Talk to us about invoicing”** for procurement. Public acquisition should follow the readiness gates. Run small, capped exact- and phrase-match search campaigns on those two intents after tracking connects landing page → trial workspace → verified route → paid workspace → second paid month. Compare each page’s cost per retained workspace against contribution margin and an agreed payback period. At $79 and the illustrative 70% margin, a three-month payback ceiling is about **$166 in acquisition cost per retained workspace**. Use that ceiling to set the campaign budget and check it against observed ad prices. The first purchase worth scaling is a manager renewing after the desk has relied on Premium Team for a real shift change.
